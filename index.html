What's actually near you

No two clusters look alike.

A vital anchor, an institutional mooring, an escale — each offered separately, by different independent people. Rarely all of them in one place. Often just one. What's around you is what's around you.

Vital anchor

A tent, a tarp, a caravan, a tiny house — a room, an attic, a cellar. Secure enough to leave a bag, a wallet, a passport, and find them untouched. Steady enough to return to, night after night, without needing to explain yourself.

Institutional mooring

An address, a desk, sometimes a vehicle. What lets you exist on paper — receive mail, renew a document, be legitimate.

Escale

Not just a shower. A table, a sofa, a chair — warm, hybrid, made to sit and stay a while: to work, store, dry laundry, cook, depending which.

A cluster starts with one vital anchor. Add another nearby — ideally two or three, anywhere from a hundred meters to a kilometer or two — and it becomes an operational cluster: what starts to feel like a distributed foyer, a home spread across several addresses instead of one.

The three escales

Waypoints, not amenities.

An escale is a stop along the way — warm, comforting, hybrid: always a table, a chair, a sofa you can stretch out on. What you do there depends which one it is. Never overnight — that part stays with the vital anchor. Three escales — work, storage, kitchen — each broken down below.

Naming

Why "Clusters"

A cluster, most literally, is a bunch of grapes — separate grains, held close together by nothing but proximity. Not amenities: no pool, no coworking lounge. Modest heating, simple showers, functional grade from day one.

For providers

Provide a service, not a listing.

Clusters curates who's admitted. A provider offers a vital anchor, an institutional mooring, or an escale — alone or combined: a shower, a desk, a spot to camp. The standard is functional-grade — no superfluous comfort — not luxury.
